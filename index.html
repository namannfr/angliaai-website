Assessment infrastructure for defensible spoken English

AI assessment is easy to demonstrate. Defensible assessment is the harder problem.

AngliaAI is being developed to conduct and score live spoken-English assessments against your organisation’s own rubric, combining AI, certified human examiners and continuous assurance, so that a score comes with the evidence that it can be trusted.

Built for universities, awarding bodies and regulators that own or operate their own English assessment standards.

The problem

Speaking assessment still depends on difficult human judgement.

The speaking component is the hardest and most expensive part of an English assessment to run. Organisations also need to show why each result should be trusted.

One examiner, one candidate
A trained examiner sits with one candidate at a time, holds a live conversation and marks it by hand.
Examiners are scarce
Capacity depends on recruiting, training and standardising people who are hard to find.
Scheduling is a bottleneck
Assessment windows are set by examiner availability, and results take time.
Judgements vary
Two examiners can score the same candidate differently, and one examiner can vary from day to day.
The challenge is no longer simply producing a score. It is proving that the score deserves to be trusted.

In one peer-reviewed study of an institutional speaking test, only 36% of score variation came from real differences between candidates. 43% came from disagreement between raters and 21% from raters disagreeing with themselves.[2]

The study covered three untrained raters in one institution. It illustrates the risk where marking is not standardised; it is not a sector-wide estimate.

Regulatory pressure

A regulator has acted on undetected marking errors

In June 2026 Ofqual fined Cambridge English £875,000 in respect of IELTS. Automated marking failures went undetected from August 2023 to September 2025, and 62,794 test takers received incorrect results. The stated cause was weaknesses in monitoring and error detection.[1]

Institutional demand

UK institutions already run their own English assessment

685,565 overseas students were at UK higher education providers in 2024/25, including 404,555 new entrants.[4] Many providers run in-house tests, entry diagnostics and pre-sessional exit assessments under their own standards.

Sources & evidence
  1. [4]House of Commons Library, CBP-7976: International students in UK higher education; HESA 2024/25 statistics. View source

Government-scale infrastructure

The state is procuring digital English assessment at scale

The Home Office English Language Test (HOELT) procurement is valued at £680m excluding VAT over five years.[5] In March 2026 the IELTS partnership withdrew from the tender, citing security, quality and integrity standards.[6] We cite this as evidence of scale. AngliaAI is not involved in the procurement.

Sources & evidence

Assessment integrity

Independent scrutiny of English tests is growing

An independent benchmarking body for English language tests launched in 2025.[7] Published research also reports that AI speaking assessment performs less well on spontaneous speech than on controlled tasks.[3] Organisations increasingly need systems that are consistent, explainable and auditable.

How AngliaAI works

One assessment. Five layers of trust.

Our proposed architecture treats every assessment as a single object that carries its conversation, score, confidence, human decision and version history together.

Assurance

A confidence gate, standard-drift monitoring, cohort fairness monitoring and generation of the validity evidence pack.

Why it exists

This layer is designed to turn a score into a decision an institution can defend to its own quality committee or auditor.

  • Confidence gate
  • Drift monitoring
  • Fairness monitoring
  • Evidence pack

  1. L1Conversation

    Scores against the client’s own rubric through a versioned calibration profile built from the client’s marked exemplars.

    • Calibration profile

Why AngliaAI

Five differentiators, built around assurance.

  1. 01

    Assurance as the product

    Evidence is part of the commercial proposition. Each deployment is designed to produce a validity pack: agreement with certified examiners, reliability, distribution alignment and fairness, refreshed each cycle.

  2. 02

    Client-owned standard

    The organisation keeps its own standard, calibration profile and candidate data. AngliaAI provides the engine. Neutrality is intended to be contractual.

  3. 03

    Interaction, not read-aloud

    The platform is designed around genuine two-way spoken interaction: topic development, repair, listener responsiveness and turn management, scored at discourse level.

  4. 04

    Examiner-grade standardisation

    Human examiners remain part of the system. The first product places the engine behind examiners for second marking, drift detection and consistency reporting.

  5. 05

    Compounding ground truth

    Low-confidence cases are reviewed by certified examiners, creating adjudicated reference data that can improve future calibration.
