Assurance

The score is only part of the product.

The assurance layer is what the rest of the platform exists to support. It is designed to turn a score into a decision an institution can defend to its own quality committee, auditor or regulator.

Development stage: pre-pilot · what this means

What it is designed to answer

Seven questions a quality officer should be able to answer without calling the vendor.

  1. Agreement

    How closely does the engine agree with certified human examiners?

  2. Drift

    Has the client’s standard drifted?

  3. Fairness

    Are different cohorts being treated consistently?

  4. Confidence

    What confidence does the engine have in a decision?

  5. Escalation

    Which cases require human intervention?

  6. Reconstruction

    Can a historic decision be reconstructed?

  7. Reporting

    What evidence can be given to an internal quality committee or auditor?

The Evidence Pack

A report your standards committee can read for itself.

Each deployment is designed to produce a validity and fairness pack for every cohort: agreement statistics, reliability indicators, distribution alignment and fairness analysis, stamped with the engine, calibration and rubric versions that produced it.

Design principle

Never hide a limitation.

A report that only ever contains good news is of little use where trust is the point.

The confidence level appears beside every score. Cohort fairness flags are shown as prominently as favourable findings. An assessment the engine could not resolve is declared, not smoothed over.

The reason is practical. In the 2026 Ofqual case, the stated cause was weakness in monitoring and error detection, not an inability to score.[1] A system that surfaces its own uncertainty is easier to supervise.

Trust, governance and responsible AI

Designed with these requirements in mind.

These are design commitments for the product. They are not statements of legal or regulatory compliance.

Human-in-the-loop assessment
Certified examiners are a permanent part of the design, not a transitional step.
Confidence-based escalation
Low-confidence results are routed to an examiner before release.
Fairness monitoring
Score distributions are analysed by declared cohort characteristics, and adverse findings are disclosed to the client.
Version-controlled calibration
Each calibration profile is versioned, and changes are approved by the client’s quality owner.
Auditability
Each state change records the actor, the time and the engine and profile versions.
Data ownership
The client owns its standard, its calibration profile and its candidate data.
Candidate transparency
Candidates can see what is recorded, what is assessed and the reasoning behind a result.
Appeals and re-marking
The appeal route and its timescale are published when the result is issued.
Reasonable adjustments
An active adjustment is shown to the candidate, with a statement that it does not affect the score.
Data protection
The design takes account of UK rules on automated individual decision-making.[8]
Long-term record integrity
A released result is superseded, never edited, so earlier versions remain inspectable.

AngliaAI makes no claim of regulatory approval, certification or compliance. The regulatory position of each deployment will be established with the client.
